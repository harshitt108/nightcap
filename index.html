Why a wait, not a wall

Late-night scrolling isn’t a willpower problem. It’s a friction problem.
A hard block
starts a fight. You want the app, it’s gone, so you switch the blocker off — and now nothing protects you.
A wait
doesn’t fight you. It puts a couple of minutes between the urge and the app, and you stay the one who decides.
No shame
No streaks to lose, no “you failed”, no lectures. Just a calm “It’s time to rest.”

Questions

Good to know

Can I still use a paused app if I really need it?

Always. Tap Emergency Unlock, wait out the timer, and open the app for a short break. Nightcap makes the distraction wait — it never takes the choice away.

Will it ever block calls or an emergency?

No. Your phone, messages, alarm clock, Settings and emergency services are never paused, whatever you select.

Can I switch it off at 2 AM?

Not on a whim. While a night is running, the wait, the break, your apps and your schedule can only be made stricter — you decide in the evening, not when you’re tired. If you really need to change something, type a short sentence, wait the same time as Emergency Unlock, and your settings open for 10 minutes. Your phone’s own settings always let you remove Nightcap.

Does Nightcap track my sleep?

No. It doesn’t measure sleep or anything about your body. It counts how often a paused app was reached for and what you chose — on your phone only.

What permissions does it need?

Two, on Android: Usage Access, to see which app is on screen, and Display over other apps, to show the pause screen on top of it. Notifications are optional — a bedtime heads-up, a note when your break is ready, and morning and weekly summaries, all scheduled on your phone. Nothing it sees leaves your phone.

Is there an iPhone version?

Later. Android comes first; iPhone needs Apple’s Screen Time approval, which we’ll apply for next.
